# 1.4.5 Images of Text

## Learning objectives

Recognize images that contain text and choose real text when WCAG 2.2 criterion 1.4.5 (Level AA) applies: https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#images-of-text.

## Why real text is more adaptable

An image containing words fixes their appearance into pixels or a graphic. People may be unable to change its font, spacing, foreground, or background to suit their needs. Real text can usually respond to browser zoom and user styles, be selected, translated, and presented by assistive technology. Alternative text is important for an image, but it does not make the visible words themselves as adaptable as real text.

When the technologies being used can achieve the visual presentation, use text rather than images of text to convey information. The criterion allows images of text that can be visually customized to the user's requirements, and cases where a particular presentation is essential to the information being conveyed. Logotypes are considered essential. Consult the full criterion for its precise scope; do not assume that a designer's preference makes an image essential.

## Replace a graphic heading

```html
<h1 class="hero-title">Welcome to the course</h1>
```

```css
.hero-title { font-size: 2rem; font-weight: 700; }
```

This heading can adapt to text settings. Check contrast, resizing, wrapping, and the font's legibility. If a screenshot is needed to teach an application's appearance, describe its relevant information in surrounding text or an alternative; assess whether its specific presentation is essential in that context. A photograph that incidentally contains a street sign is not necessarily a designed image of text used to convey the page's message; evaluate its role rather than assuming every visible word is a failure.

## Identify the function of the graphic

A marketing banner says “Enrollment closes Friday” as a flattened image. The wording is the message, and the same visual presentation could be made with HTML and CSS. Replacing it with a heading or paragraph lets users select and adapt the text. A text alternative on the image may help a screen-reader user understand the message, but the visual words remain fixed inside the graphic for someone who needs different spacing or colors.

By contrast, a screenshot in a lesson about using a particular application may need to show the application's actual appearance. Ask what information must be conveyed and whether that particular visual representation is essential. Even if an image is justified, provide a text alternative and surrounding explanation that convey relevant information. An exception from this criterion does not remove every other accessibility obligation.

## Customization and essential presentation

The criterion permits an image of text if it can be visually customized to the user's requirements, or if a particular presentation is essential to the information being conveyed. Assess the actual customization offered: a single fixed graphic is not customizable merely because a designer can edit its source file. Users should be able to make the needed visual adjustments in their experience. A logo is explicitly treated as essential, but the exception should not be extended automatically to an entire page of decorative headings.

Some fonts or treatments may be difficult to reproduce exactly. The question is whether the technology can achieve the intended visual presentation and whether the precise presentation is essential, not whether an exported image is more convenient for the author. Where practical, use actual text styled with CSS, including a suitable web font and graceful fallbacks. Check that user style overrides and larger text do not destroy the layout.

## Comparison and review

An image-based button labeled “Continue” needs alternative text and keyboard functionality, but a native button containing real text is usually easier to adapt and operate. Test its accessible name, visible label, contrast, focus, and resizing. A photograph containing an incidental shop sign needs context-based evaluation; do not classify the entire photograph as a failure just because words appear inside it.

Review graphics in the content management system, including images uploaded by authors, not just those in the codebase. For each relevant image, note its purpose, whether words are being used to convey information, and the reason for any exception. Confirm that alternative content remains available if an image does not load. Accessibility tests should cover the actual published content and its responsive states.

## Design review: course cards

A course catalog uses a different image for each card title. When the title changes, the author exports a new graphic with the words baked in. A user who increases text size sees the image enlarge as a picture, but cannot adjust the letters independently, and a narrow crop can cut off part of the title. Put the course name in a real heading and use the graphic as a background or adjacent decorative image. If the image itself adds information, give it an appropriate alternative or explain that information in the card.

A native heading also gives the document a useful structure, but use the correct heading level for its place on the page rather than choosing a level solely for its default size. Style the heading visually with CSS. Test the resulting card with zoom, user styles, and a longer title. A background image can reduce contrast, so use a solid text background where necessary and measure it.

If an author argues that a particular lettering treatment is essential, ask what information would be lost with real text and document the reasoning. The criterion's essential exception applies to cases where the specific presentation is essential to the conveyed information, not merely where the image is a convenient export. Evaluate the exact context and provide alternatives for content users cannot perceive.

## Required lesson activity

Inventory a page's graphic headings, banners, logos, and screenshots. For each, explain whether the words communicate information and whether real text can reproduce the presentation. Replace one nonessential graphic heading with HTML and CSS. Test selection, zoom, contrast, and accessibility-tree output; document any claimed exception.

## Mini-assessment

1. Why can real text be more usable than a graphic heading? Answer: Users can often change its size and presentation more easily.
2. Does alternative text alone make the visible text in an image resizable as text? Answer: No.
3. Is a logotype considered essential for this criterion? Answer: Yes; consult the criterion for the exception's scope.
