# 1.2.8 Media Alternative (Prerecorded)

## Learning objectives

Explain the scope of a media alternative for prerecorded content, distinguish it from a speech-only transcript, and produce an equivalent sequence of information. This is a Level AAA criterion.

## What the alternative must cover

The criterion requires an alternative for time-based media for prerecorded synchronized media and prerecorded video-only media. The alternative communicates the information needed to understand the original without relying on the media's timing. For synchronized media, this includes meaningful visual and audio information. For video-only media, describe the visual content. Organize the information in the same meaningful sequence, and provide a means to achieve interactions when required by the definition of an alternative for time-based media.

Consider a tutorial where a narrator speaks, selects a setting, and the application silently displays an error. A transcript containing only the narration omits the selection and error. A more complete alternative includes the spoken instructions, the relevant selection, the error text, and the result in order. Speaker changes and significant sounds may also matter.

## Create and verify the alternative

Review the original with both audio and visual information available. Outline its meaningful events, then write the content so a person can understand the sequence without playing it. Include spoken information, descriptions of visual actions and settings, relevant sounds, and any interactive opportunities as appropriate. Exclude incidental decoration that contributes nothing to meaning.

Ask a reviewer to complete the task using only the alternative, then compare their understanding with the original. Fix omissions, incorrect ordering, and ambiguous references. Provide a discoverable link to the alternative and check its own accessibility. A one-sentence summary, caption file containing only audio, or list of topics cannot be assumed equivalent to the full media content.

## Relationship to other criteria

This Level AAA criterion has a different scope from 1.2.3, which permits a choice between audio description and an alternative for some prerecorded synchronized media at Level A. A text alternative does not remove separate caption or audio-description requirements at their respective levels. Review the W3C definition and applicable criteria for the actual media.

## Worked example: an instructional recording

A recording begins with a presenter explaining a search, shows the presenter selecting a filter, plays a short alert sound, and displays “No results.” The presenter then changes the filter and receives three matches. A speech transcript alone may contain the explanation but omit the selected filter, the alert, and the change in results.

An equivalent alternative can present those events in order: identify the speaker, reproduce the relevant explanation, describe the filter selected and the displayed message, note the meaningful alert, and describe the revised search and result. If the original enables interaction, account for the opportunity to perform an equivalent interaction according to the definition. A short paragraph saying “This video teaches search” only describes the topic and cannot establish equivalence.

## Check the alternative against the source

Make two passes through the recording: one for visual actions and text, and one for dialogue and significant sounds. Merge the notes into a coherent sequence. Ask someone who has not watched the recording to follow the instructions and report the result. Compare their response with the source and correct missing steps or ambiguous references. Verify the alternative link is available to learners and the alternative itself can be navigated. Keep the source and alternative synchronized when the recording changes.

## Preserve interactions and sequence

Imagine a recorded instructional presentation that pauses and asks learners to select an answer before showing feedback. A description that merely reproduces the feedback skips the decision the learner was meant to make. The definition of an alternative for time-based media includes a means to achieve the outcomes of time-based interaction. An accessible alternative might present the question, offer an equivalent way to answer, and then provide the relevant feedback. The implementation must be evaluated for the actual interaction and outcome.

For a noninteractive recording, the key challenge may be sequence. If an error appears before the presenter corrects it, placing the error description at the end can make the correction puzzling. Preserve the order of the important action, error, and recovery. Give meaningful screen text rather than replacing every message with “a notification appears.” At the same time, avoid adding a supposed reason for an error that the source never gives.

Maintain the alternative when the original changes. A revised tutorial might move a step, change a button label, or add an important warning. Compare the entire alternative with the final published recording, not only the original script. Verify the link to it and test whether a learner can follow the process using the alternative alone.

## Required lesson activity

Create an alternative for a short narrated screen recording. Include narration, relevant screen text, actions, outcomes, and any meaningful sounds in sequence. Have another student use only the alternative to describe the procedure; revise at least one ambiguity or omission and explain the change.

## Mini-assessment

1. Which prerecorded media types are covered by 1.2.8?
2. Why may a speech-only transcript be insufficient for a screen recording?
3. What should you do if the original media offers an interaction?

Suggested answers: Prerecorded synchronized and video-only media; it can omit meaningful visual actions or results; provide a means to achieve equivalent interaction as required by the definition.
