# 1.4.9 Images of Text (No Exception)

## Learning objectives

Recognize images of text and describe the Level AAA restriction. Read [WCAG 2.2 criterion 1.4.9](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#images-of-text-no-exception).

## Preserve text as text

Text embedded in an image often cannot be restyled, resized, translated, or reflowed as effectively as actual text. For this criterion, images of text are used only for pure decoration or where a particular presentation of text is essential to the information conveyed. Logotypes, meaning text that is part of a logo or brand name, are considered essential.

A promotional banner whose image contains “Register today” can instead use HTML text over a background with CSS. The text can then adapt to user settings. A photograph that incidentally includes a street sign differs from an image whose primary purpose is presenting stylized text. Consider context and whether the appearance is essential; a convenient design choice alone does not establish essentiality. Providing alternative text can help communicate an image, but does not by itself meet this restriction on images of text.

## Compare the AA and AAA requirements

Criterion 1.4.5 addresses images of text at Level AA when the technologies used can achieve the visual presentation, and includes a customizable-image exception. At Level AAA, 1.4.9 limits images of text to pure decoration or a particular presentation essential to the information conveyed. Customization is not a separate general exception under 1.4.9. Consult each criterion's exact wording rather than assuming they have identical conditions.

Logotypes are explicitly considered essential. A company logo may contain stylized lettering, but this does not automatically make a promotional sentence beside the logo essential as an image. Put the sentence in real text where the exact graphical representation is not essential. The criterion concerns the purpose and context of the image, not simply whether a designer prefers the graphic.

## Worked scenario: an event banner

A flattened banner contains an event name, a date, and “Register now.” On a narrow screen, the image shrinks and the date becomes hard to read. An alt attribute may convey the words to a screen reader, but the visible letters remain part of an image and cannot be restyled independently. Replace the message with a real heading, date, and link in HTML, and use CSS for its visual treatment. Retain the picture as a background or adjacent image if it contributes to the design; describe it if it adds relevant information.

After the change, test that the text wraps at larger sizes, remains readable over the background, and responds to user color settings. Check that the link has a clear purpose and can be operated. These tests address related requirements: using real text does not automatically establish sufficient contrast or keyboard accessibility.

## Classify content carefully

A photograph of a street may incidentally include a sign. Evaluate whether the author is using the words as text content and whether the sign's wording matters to the lesson or task. If it matters, convey it in suitable surrounding text or an alternative. A screenshot illustrating a specific interface may have an essential visual purpose, but still needs an explanation for people who cannot perceive it.

For each graphic in an audit, record the words, the purpose, and any reason a particular presentation is essential or purely decorative. Ask what information would be lost if the words were real text. Convenience of export, or a desire to preserve an exact typeface, does not by itself prove essentiality. Review the actual published page at different viewport sizes and document any unresolved case.

## A practical replacement

A promotional card can preserve its artwork while moving its message into the document:

```html
<article class="event-card">
  <h2>Accessibility workshop</h2>
  <p>Registration closes Friday.</p>
  <a href="/register">Register for the workshop</a>
</article>
```

The background can be styled with CSS, provided the text remains legible against the actual image. The heading and link are selectable text and can respond to user settings. Check the result at large text sizes and on a narrow screen. If the artwork contributes unique information, provide an appropriate alternative rather than treating it as automatically decorative. A real-text replacement should preserve the original message and the action, not silently remove either.

## Review authoring workflows

A content management system may let authors upload a complete banner image with its wording already embedded. Even if the template supports accessible HTML, the published result can reintroduce the problem. Provide a separate title, description, link label, and image field so authors can add the message as text. Review existing content and explain why the text field is needed. Avoid placing duplicate visible words in both an image and HTML unless the experience remains clear to everyone.

Ask whether an apparently decorative graphic is actually the only place a date or offer appears. If it conveys useful information, it is not pure decoration merely because it is visually attractive. Conversely, an ornamental graphic containing meaningless stylized marks may be decorative. For a claimed essential case, explain the connection between the exact presentation and the information conveyed; consult the criterion when uncertain.

The AAA restriction is stricter than the customizable-image allowance at AA. Providing a user control to change the appearance of an image may be relevant to 1.4.5, but it is not a separate general exception in 1.4.9. Inspect alternatives, contrast, and responsive behavior as separate parts of the page review.

## Required lesson activity

Inspect a page with a logo, a promotional banner containing text, and a photograph with incidental lettering. Classify each and replace the banner's text with HTML and CSS. Test zoom and user color settings. Explain your decisions and remaining questions.

## Mini-assessment

1. Are logotypes considered essential under this criterion?
2. Does adding alt text automatically make an image of promotional text meet 1.4.9?
3. What implementation can replace text embedded in a banner image?

**Answer guide:** 1. Yes. 2. No. 3. Actual HTML text styled with CSS can provide the same message.
